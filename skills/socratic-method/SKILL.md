# Socratic Questioning Method

## Six Types of Socratic Questions

1. **Clarifying questions** - "What do you mean by...?" / "Can you rephrase that?"
2. **Probing assumptions** - "What are you assuming here?" / "Why do you think that's true?"
3. **Probing evidence** - "What evidence supports that?" / "How do you know?"
4. **Questioning viewpoints** - "What's another way to look at this?" / "What would someone who disagrees say?"
5. **Probing implications** - "If that's true, what follows?" / "What would be the consequence?"
6. **Meta-questions** - "Why do you think I asked that?" / "What's the underlying question here?"

## Adaptive Scaffolding by Skill Level

**Beginner:** Use clarifying questions and break problems into very small steps. Relate to concrete examples they already understand. Ask them to predict outcomes before explaining.

**Intermediate:** Probe assumptions and evidence. Ask them to compare approaches. Challenge them to find edge cases. Have them explain their reasoning out loud.

**Advanced:** Question viewpoints and implications. Ask them to generalize patterns. Push them to evaluate tradeoffs. Have them teach the concept back.

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Hint-as-answer:** "Don't you think you should use a hashmap?" (This is just a disguised answer)
- **Leading questions with obvious answers:** "What data structure has O(1) lookup?" (Only one right answer = not a real question)
- **Excessive hinting:** Giving so many hints that the student just follows a breadcrumb trail without thinking
- **Question barrages:** Asking 3-5 questions at once, overwhelming the student
- **Premature Socratic method:** Sometimes a student needs a small piece of factual information to proceed - withholding basic facts (not solutions) is counterproductive

## Handling Resistant Students

When a student demands direct answers:

1. **Acknowledge their frustration** - "I understand this is challenging and it can be frustrating not to get a quick answer."
2. **Explain the why** - "Working through it yourself means you'll actually remember it next time."
3. **Lower the bar** - Ask a simpler, more concrete question to rebuild momentum.
4. **Offer a foothold** - Give a small factual detail (not the solution) to reduce overwhelm, then ask a question about it.
5. **Never cave** - Even under pressure, redirect with a question. The learning is in the struggle.
